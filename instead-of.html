popular products, kinder swaps

Some of the biggest names on the shelf are still not cruelty-free — not because of a rumour or an old boycott, but because their parent company sells into markets like mainland China, where animal testing on cosmetics can still be legally required, and hasn't adopted a policy that rules it out everywhere. Here are eleven you've probably got in your bathroom or under your sink right now, each paired with a genuinely certified alternative that does the same job.

Every brand below, on both sides, is pulled straight from my brand check database — nothing here is a one-off claim, you can look each one up yourself.

a note on brands I've left out

Dove and Herbal Essences are both PETA-certified cruelty-free in their own right, even though their parent companies (Unilever and P&G) sell elsewhere in ways that don't meet that standard — that's a genuinely different, more mixed situation than the brands above, so I've left them off this specific list rather than force them into a simple "not cruelty-free" box.
