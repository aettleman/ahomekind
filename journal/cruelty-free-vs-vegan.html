Cruelty-Free vs Vegan: What's the Difference?

"Cruelty-free" and "vegan" get used together so often that most people assume they're two words for the same thing.

They're not, and the difference actually matters.

Cruelty-free is about testing. A cruelty-free product or brand hasn't used animals to test its products or ingredients. It says nothing about what's actually in the bottle.

Vegan is about ingredients. A vegan product doesn't contain anything derived from animals or animal exploitation, which covers the obvious stuff and the less obvious stuff too, things like beeswax, lanolin, carmine, or certain kinds of collagen that don't announce themselves on the front of the packaging.

Put those two together and you get four possible combinations, not one. A product can be cruelty-free and vegan, which is the good outcome everyone's picturing. It can be cruelty-free but not vegan, tested on nobody but still made with an animal-derived ingredient. Or, and this is the one that catches people out hardest, it can be entirely vegan and still not cruelty-free, made from zero animal ingredients while the company behind it pays for tests elsewhere. Vegan doesn't guarantee cruelty-free, and cruelty-free doesn't guarantee vegan. Neither word is doing the other one's job.

Brands complicate this further, because a brand can have some vegan products without being a fully vegan brand. That's why I don't slap a blanket "vegan" label on a whole company, I mark 100% vegan brands and "some products are vegan" brands as two different things, because treating them the same would just be lying to you slightly less obviously than the brands do.

It's also why, when you look a brand up here, cruelty-free status, vegan status, parent company and the evidence behind it all show up separately rather than getting mashed into one tidy verdict. Maybe animal testing is the thing you actually care about. Maybe it's the ingredients. Maybe it's both. There's no single "correct" way to shop, so I'd rather hand you the pieces and let you decide what matters to you than pretend there's one right answer for everyone.

None of this requires memorising an ingredients glossary before you're allowed to buy shampoo. That's the entire point of doing the digging here, so you don't have to become an expert just to make a slightly kinder choice.
