Cruelty-Free vs Vegan: What's the Difference?

h1>

The words "cruelty-free" and "vegan" often appear next to each other.

It's easy to assume they mean basically the same thing.

They don't.

🐰 Cruelty-free

Cruelty-free generally refers to animal testing.

A cruelty-free product or brand doesn't use animals in testing its products or ingredients, according to the standards being applied.

But that doesn't necessarily tell us what the product is made from.

🌱 Vegan

Vegan generally refers to the ingredients and materials used.

A vegan product doesn't contain ingredients derived from animals or animal exploitation, according to the definition being used.

That means things such as beeswax, lanolin, carmine or certain forms of animal-derived collagen can be relevant when determining whether a product is vegan.

Here's where it gets interesting...

A product can be:

🐰 Cruelty-free + 🌱 Vegan

Great. It meets both criteria.

But it can also be:

🐰 Cruelty-free + ❌ Not vegan

For example, a product might not be tested on animals but contain an animal-derived ingredient.

Or:

❌ Not cruelty-free + 🌱 Vegan

A product could contain no animal-derived ingredients but still be associated with animal testing.

So vegan does not automatically mean cruelty-free, and cruelty-free does not automatically mean vegan.

🧴 What about brands?

This gets even more complicated because a brand can have some vegan products without being a completely vegan brand.
