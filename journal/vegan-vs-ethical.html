Vegan Isn't the Same as Ethical

Vegan and ethical get used as if they mean the same thing. They overlap, but they are not the same question, and the gap between them is where a lot of greenwashing lives.

Vegan asks one thing: were animals used to make this. It is a clear question with a checkable answer, which is exactly why it works so well as a label. You can look at an ingredients list and find out.

Ethical is not one question. It is a pile of them. Who grew it, who picked it, what were they paid, what happened to the land it came from, who made the packaging, how far did it travel, and who profits at the end. No label on a bottle can answer all of that, and any label claiming to is selling you something.

Palm oil is the example that makes this obvious. It is entirely vegan. It is also one of the biggest drivers of rainforest clearance in Indonesia and Malaysia, and the orangutans, elephants and tigers losing that habitat are not comforted by the fact that no animal product went into the bottle. A vegan shampoo with unsustainable palm oil in it has a body count. It just isn't printed on the label.

There are others. Cashews are often shelled by hand, and the shells release a caustic sap that burns skin, frequently for very low pay. Almonds are thirsty in places that are running out of water. Most vegan leather is plastic, which solves the cow and creates a different problem that outlives all of us. None of this makes those things indefensible, but it does mean "vegan" and "harmless" are not synonyms.

It runs the other way too. A small soap maker down the road using beeswax from their own hives is not vegan, and may still do less damage than a global vegan brand shipping plastic bottles across an ocean. I find that genuinely uncomfortable, and I would rather say so than pretend the tidy answer is always the right one.

Vegan is a floor, not a ceiling.

Here is where I land. Vegan is a floor. It rules out a whole category of harm, cleanly and verifiably, and that is worth a great deal. It is the single most useful line you can draw if you want one line. But it is a floor, not a ceiling, and treating it as the finish line is how you end up with a bathroom full of vegan products from companies you would not defend if you looked into them.

The thing I want to avoid saying is "so it is all impossible, do what you like". It is not impossible. It is just layered. You can be vegan and also look at who owns the brand. You can be vegan and also buy less. You can be vegan and still ask where the palm oil came from. Each of those is a separate check, and you do not have to do all of them on the same afternoon.

What I would not do is let the complexity talk you out of the simple part. Somebody pointing out that almonds use water is not making an argument for dairy. Somebody pointing out that vegan leather is plastic is not making an argument for leather. Those points are worth taking seriously on their own terms, and they are also, very often, being made by people who would rather you did nothing at all.

So: vegan answers one question, and answers it well. Ask the others too, when you have the room for them. Not all at once, and not as a reason to stop.
