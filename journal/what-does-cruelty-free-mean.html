What Does "Cruelty-Free" Actually Mean?

When you see the words "cruelty-free" on a product, it sounds pretty straightforward.

No animal testing. Simple, right?

Well… unfortunately, it's a little more complicated than that.

There isn't one universal legal definition of "cruelty-free" that every brand has to meet. Different organisations and certifications have their own standards, and brands can use different language to describe their animal-testing policies.

That's why checking whether something is genuinely cruelty-free can sometimes feel like detective work.

🐰 So what does cruelty-free actually mean?

Generally, when we describe a brand or product as cruelty-free, we're talking about whether animals have been used in testing the product, its ingredients, or its development.

That can include testing carried out directly by a company, or testing carried out on its behalf by suppliers or other organisations.

But there's an important distinction:

Cruelty-free does not automatically mean vegan.

A product can contain animal-derived ingredients while never having been tested on animals.

And a product can be made entirely from plant-based or synthetic ingredients while being sold by a company that isn't considered cruelty-free.

We'll get into that distinction in more detail in our guide to cruelty-free vs vegan.

🔎 Why isn't it always obvious?

Because a brand saying something like:

"We don't test our finished products on animals"

doesn't necessarily tell you everything you need to know.

You may also want to know about:

  • Ingredients and raw materials
  • Suppliers
  • Third-party testing
  • The company's wider animal-testing policy
  • Whether the brand is independently certified
  • Its parent company
  • Where and how its products are sold

That's why Home Kind doesn't simply look for a cute bunny logo and call it a day.

We want to make the information easier to understand so you can make your own decision.

🏢 What about parent companies?

This is one of the most confusing parts of cruelty-free shopping.

A cruelty-free brand can be owned by a larger company whose wider policies don't meet the same standard.

For example, a brand may have its own cruelty-free certification while being owned by a parent company with a different animal-testing policy.

Home Kind therefore shows both the brand's status and its parent company where relevant, rather than hiding that information.

That doesn't mean we're telling you what you must buy.

It's about giving you enough information to decide what feels right to you.

🏅 What do cruelty-free certifications mean?

Independent certification can provide additional reassurance because the organisation behind the certification has its own criteria.

Examples include recognised programmes such as Leaping Bunny and PETA's Beauty Without Bunnies.

However, certifications aren't the only thing worth looking at.

A good cruelty-free check should consider the available evidence and the specific circumstances surrounding the brand.
