fashion & accessories

Cruelty-free here means something different than it does on the rest of this site. With cosmetics, it's about whether the company tested on animals. With clothes, it's about whether the material itself is animal-derived, leather, wool, silk, down, fur, exotic skins, regardless of the brand's testing policy. A brand can be genuinely fur-free and still sell plenty of leather and wool, so I've kept the two questions separate rather than trying to squeeze fashion into the same tier system used for brand-check.

Being fur-free is genuinely good. It is not the same as being cruelty-free.

where major UK retailers actually stand

Being fur-free is genuinely good, but it's not the same as being fully cruelty-free in the way that phrase is used elsewhere on this site, since most fur-free brands still sell leather, wool and down. I've noted where a policy is a self-declared corporate commitment versus backed by a third-party programme like Fur Free Retailer, since neither of these is independently audited the way Leaping Bunny certifies cosmetics, so I'd treat all of them as good-faith commitments rather than guarantees.
