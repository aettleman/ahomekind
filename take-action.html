write to your MP

An MP is obliged to read correspondence from people in their own constituency, and volume genuinely registers. It takes about two minutes. You don't need to be an expert, and you don't need to write anything clever. Your own words in a short, polite message count for more than a long one copied word for word, so change whatever you like below.

find your MP and send this straight to them

Opens WriteToThem in a new tab, a free, independent service that looks up your MP from your postcode and sends your message on to them directly, no email app needed. Parliament's own lookup works too if you'd rather email your MP yourself.

cosmetics testing

On the gap between the cosmetics testing ban and what still happens

The UK banned animal testing for cosmetics in 1998, and for cosmetic ingredients in 1998 as well. But ingredients used in cosmetics can still be tested on animals when they're assessed under chemicals regulation (REACH) rather than as cosmetics, which is the gap this asks about. Licences of that kind have been granted in recent years.

Dear [MP's name],

I am writing as one of your constituents in [your constituency].

The UK is rightly proud of having banned animal testing for cosmetics and cosmetic ingredients. However, I understand that ingredients destined for cosmetics can still be tested on animals when they are assessed for chemical safety under REACH rather than under cosmetics rules, and that licences permitting this have been granted in recent years.

I would like to know what the Government's position is on closing that gap, so that the ban means in practice what the public understands it to mean. I would also welcome your view on the funding and regulatory acceptance of non-animal testing methods, which remain the route to ending these procedures altogether.

I would be grateful if you could let me know your own view, and whether you intend to raise this with the relevant minister.

Thank you for your time.

Yours sincerely,
[your name]
[your address, including postcode]

Change anything you like before sending. A letter in your own words is worth more than a perfect one that reads like a form.
